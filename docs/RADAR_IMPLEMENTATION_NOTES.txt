ALTA RADAR MODEL IMPLEMENTATION PROVENANCE

The benchmark contains compact local adaptations, not author checkpoints.
No published-paper accuracy is implied by the model-family names.

ConvLSTM: two spatial scales, convolutional input/state LSTM transitions,
encoder/forecast recurrence, softplus output and locked channel dropout.
Original 2015 source:
https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2015/file/07563a3fe3bbe7e3ba84431ad9d055af-Paper.pdf

DGMR adaptation: four-scale context stack; pixel unshuffle/shuffle; residual
spectrally normalized blocks; stochastic latent stack with spatial attention;
four recurrent ConvGRU decoder scales; separate spatial and temporal
adversarial discriminators; hinge adversarial loss and ensemble-mean weighted
reconstruction loss. Width reduced to base8, log1p precipitation training,
nine10-minute input frames,20future frames (through200minutes fromlastinput),
and two rather than six/four ensemble members during training. Common3-hour
verification starts after the latency/reporting alignment chosen bycaller.
The family does not represent the original90-minute5-minute DGMR experiment.
DGMR pseudocode (c) DeepMind, licensed CC-BY4.0; adapted mechanisms and reduced
network widths in src/alta_radar_models.py. Source attribution Ravuri etal2021:
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03854-z
https://storage.googleapis.com/dm-nowcasting-example-data/pseudocode.zip
https://github.com/google-deepmind/deepmind-research/tree/master/nowcasting
Official inference notebook and pseudocode are preserved locally. No TensorFlow
snapshot is loaded: available environment isPython3.14 withCPU-onlyPyTorch,
while the official notebook useslegacyTensorFlow1graphs underTensorFlow2.

NowcastNet adaptation: U-Net predicts per-lead motion and growth/decay;
bilinear semi-Lagrangian transport operates in physical linear rate; a
conditional encoder/noise projector/SPADE decoder generates coherent random
trajectories; adversarial temporal discrimination and ensemble max-pool
regularization train the generative stage. Evolution is trained first and
then frozen. Width/depth, temporal discriminator and loss scales are reduced;
rate and reconstruction regularization use log1p units. This is an independent
implementation of paper mechanisms, not a verified reproduction of the
CodeOcean architecture or weights. Official capsule access could not be
verified with availablewebtools, so third-partyweights were notsubstituted.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10356617/
https://doi.org/10.24433/CO.0832447.v1

All models receive a second channel for causally available radar quality.
Unavailable target pixels are masked in loss. Cadence, channel count,
training steps, seed andvalidation-selected checkpoint are saved with eachfit.
Raw ensembles are NOT assumed calibrated probabilities of resort snowfall.
Surface snow conversion and calibrated24-hour uncertainty are separate tasks.

ADDITIONAL INDEPENDENT IMPLEMENTATION AUDIT

DGMR: the compact generator residual blocks omit the original BatchNorm,
and the compact final convolution is not spectrally normalized. Original
DGMR emits physical precipitation directly from its final linear convolution;
it does not exponentiate a log-rate output. The first two local recipes used
an exponential physical-rate parameterization. The third DGMR recipe retains
all weights/shapes and changes only the output to positive physical rate
(softplus), returning log1p(rate) to the shared training API. This fixes one
numerical amplification mechanism, not every architectural difference.

NowcastNet: the compact generator applies a bounded multiplicative correction
to the evolution forecast. At an evolution rate of zero, the generator's
0.001 mm/hour floor and residual bounded by three limit its rate to about
0.0201 mm/hour. The evolution network can create new precipitation through
its growth term, but the stochastic generator cannot independently create a
strong new cell at a location where evolution remains zero. This limitation
must not be attributed to the full published NowcastNet architecture.

Wet-case sampling reweights the training objective and applies no inverse
probability correction. Validation retains the predeclared calendar dates.
MSE of a finite ensemble mean also penalizes member variance; raw stochastic
spread is not assumed calibrated. Daily snowfall probabilities use a separate
calibration model and must be assessed on held-out surface measurements.
